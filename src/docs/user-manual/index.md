# User Manual

This manual is for the people who **use an Orchard Core site every day**: editors who write pages and posts, content managers who organize the site, site builders who shape it from the admin screens, and administrators who manage users, features and settings. It explains what each screen is for, when you would use it, and how to use it, one task at a time.

You don't need to write code or edit files to follow anything here. When a task needs a developer or your IT team, the page says so, and the **For developers and IT** box at the bottom of the page links to the [Technical Manual](../technical-manual/index.md).

## Who does what

| You are... | You usually... | Start with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Editor** | Write and update pages, blog posts and other content, add images, and publish. | [Write and publish](content/index.md) |
| **Content manager** | Organize content with lists, taxonomies and menus, translate it, and keep it findable. | [Organize content](organize/index.md) |
| **Site builder** | Create content types, choose the theme, place widgets, build forms and set up search. | [Design](design/index.md) |
| **Administrator** | Turn features on, manage users and roles, connect email and sign-in providers, and change site settings. | [Administration](administration/index.md) |

Each role has an ordered reading list in [Training paths](getting-started/training-paths.md).

!!! tip "You may not see everything described here"
    What you see in the admin depends on two things: which **features** are turned on for your site, and which **permissions** your **role** gives you. Most people are not administrators, so some menus, buttons and settings in this manual may be missing for you. Every feature page lists the menu, the permission and the feature it needs. If something is missing, see [Roles and permissions](getting-started/roles-and-permissions.md).

## Searching the manual

The search box at the top searches both manuals. To search only one, clear the other under **Search in** at the top of the results: **User Manual** or **Technical Manual**. Your choice is remembered in this browser. User Manual pages also rank higher in the results, and the browser tab of a User Manual page ends with **User Manual**, so you can tell the two apart.

## Training your team

You can use this manual to train the people who work on your site:

1. Give each person the [training path](getting-started/training-paths.md) for their role.
2. Have them repeat each task on a test content item, or on a test site set up with the **Blog** recipe (see [Set up a site](getting-started/setting-up-a-site.md)).
3. Walk them through the [use cases](use-cases/index.md) to show how the pieces fit together.
4. Keep the [glossary](glossary.md) and [troubleshooting](troubleshooting.md) pages handy for the first weeks.
